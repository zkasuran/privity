Fund trades that cannot half-settle.

The problem

Tokenized fund trades still settle like paper.

An investor wires cash. Somebody watches a bank feed. Units get typed into the register afterwards. The gap between those two events is carried by whoever is exposed when it breaks, so closing it is a manual reconciliation task every month end.

Delivery versus payment exists to remove that gap. It is not happening here. It is free delivery with a human gate.

How it works

Watch a trade settle, and watch what each party can see.

Alice sells Bob 400 of her 1,000 units for 41,000 USDC. Step through it. The flow below mirrors a real run on a Canton participant.

Alice seller
Share holding 1,000 u
Parcel, earmarked to Bob 400 u
Retained, private 600 u
Cash received 41,000
Bob buyer
Cash holding 150,000
Parcel bought 400 u
Cash change 109,000
Alice's retained units cannot see
✓ committed as one transaction, 8 events

  1. The seller earmarks exactly what it sells

    400 units split out of 1,000. That parcel, and only that parcel, becomes visible to the named buyer. The seller's remaining 600 units are a separate contract Bob is never a party to.

  2. Both legs commit together or neither does

    The share leg needs the seller's authority and the cash leg needs the buyer's. The proposal is signed by the seller and exercised by the buyer, so one transaction carries both. There is no moment where one leg has moved and the other has not.

  3. Supervision is a contract, not an arrangement

    An auditor's visibility comes from a mandate both sides sign, bounded in time and revocable with a stated reason. If only the auditor signed, anyone could assert supervision over anyone. If only the firm signed, it could manufacture evidence of an audit that never happened.

  4. NAV is checkable, not merely signed

    The administrator submits the book and the ledger derives both the NAV per unit and a canonical hash committing to those holdings. An entitled auditor recomputes both from a book it already holds. Nobody else can, and nobody learns the book from the attestation.

The design came from being wrong

The first version had the buyer read the seller's holding to verify the trade. Canton rejected it, because the buyer is not a stakeholder on that contract. The privacy property was enforced against our own code.

The tempting repair was to add the buyer as an observer. That would have disclosed the seller's entire position while still passing every happy-path test. Both versions compile. Both settle correctly. Only a test written to falsify the claim tells them apart.

So this is calibrated disclosure, not maximum secrecy. Hide everything and the buyer cannot verify what it is paying for. Disclose the holding and the product is pointless. The engineering is finding the floor, and for a secondary trade that floor is exactly one parcel.

Why Canton

Two properties that no public chain offers together.

A public chain

Gives you atomic settlement. Also publishes every investor's position to every other investor. For a regulated fund that ends the conversation.

Off-ledger

Gives you confidentiality. Also keeps cash and register as two separate events, which is the problem itself.

For uncertificated securities, prompt posting and turnaround are effectively the same event.

Morgan Lewis, summarising the SEC’s proposed transfer agent Rules 17ad-2 and 17ad-10, September 2026

Canton's sub-transaction privacy is what removes the trade-off: one transaction commits across parties while each participant validates only the views its own parties are stakeholders on. A transfer and a register update should be one event once securities stop being paper. Privity makes them literally one transaction.

Who it is for

The person who certifies the register is right.

Pain owner

Fund accounting or transfer agency lead at a mid-tier third-party administrator, 20 to 60 funds. They hold the register and produce NAV, so both legs of the problem land on one desk.

Economic buyer

Head of operations or head of fund administration. The auditor is an influencer rather than the buyer, and the fund manager is a secondary buyer.

Why they switch

Not for privacy as a feature. For a settlement that cannot half-complete, and for audit evidence produced by the process instead of reconstructed once a year.

What stops them

Any confidentiality regression is an instant no. After that, integration effort against the incumbent admin system is the real cost of adoption. Price is their smallest objection.

Honest limits

What is not done.

Stated here rather than left for a reader to find, because a project that hides its gaps makes its other claims harder to believe.

Zero practitioner interviews

Problem evidence is documentary and regulator-sourced, which moves problem validation and not market validation. The interview instrument and a named target list are built. A falsification test is committed in writing: if three interviews with the exact buyer say the timing gap is already handled well enough, the wedge is wrong and the product moves to the audit trail.

Single synchronizer, LocalNet only

A Daml transaction can only consume contracts on one synchronizer, so the atomicity here is demonstrated in that topology, which is what the Global Synchronizer provides. The cross-synchronizer case is documented by Canton, needs no change to our model, and carries no settlement risk because no value moves until the atomic step. Nothing is deployed beyond a local network.

No production integration with an admin system, which is the largest practical adoption risk and the thing we have done least about.

A design review

Should the disclosure layer be upstreamed as an ecosystem primitive? Every Canton team building a multi-party workflow hits this wall, and most reach for the observer field.
